About the Product

What is in the bottle, how it differs from fresh turmeric and other turmeric products, and what it tastes like.

What are the ingredients of a bottle of Complete 3 Turmeric™ Liquid Extract?

Each 200 ml bottle of Complete 3 Turmeric™ Liquid Extract contains 100 g of fresh turmeric rhizome (Curcuma longa) concentrated in a liquid solution.

What is the difference between fresh turmeric and Complete 3 Turmeric™ Liquid Extract?

Complete 3 Turmeric™ concentrates the beneficial compounds of a large amount of fresh turmeric into a relatively small dose. In liquid extract form it is also more easily and rapidly absorbed than turmeric consumed fresh.

Why is the fresh, whole turmeric rhizome used to make Complete 3 Turmeric™?

We endorse the holistic view that every one of a plant's natural components plays a role in optimising its therapeutic quality. Growing our own certified organic turmeric means we always select premium fresh rhizomes, so Complete 3 Turmeric™ contains the full spectrum of beneficial natural compounds.

By comparison, most turmeric extracts produced in Australia are standardised formulations made from dried powder or syrups.

Is Complete 3 Turmeric™ different to turmeric juices and beverages?

Yes. Complete 3 Turmeric™ is a listed medicine containing the complete range of fresh turmeric's active compounds, which makes it considerably more potent than cordials, elixirs, juices, carbonated drinks or coffee additives.

Those beverages are not listed medicines and generally contain turmeric for colour or flavour only, in amounts too small to deliver significant therapeutic benefit.

Does Complete 3 Turmeric™ contain curcumin concentrates, black pepper or fat supplements?

No. Complete 3 Turmeric™ does not need black pepper, because it contains turmeric's own natural oils, called turmerones, which bind with curcumin to enhance its absorption.

Supplements such as black pepper may only assist curcumin bioavailability when curcumin is isolated on its own, not when it is part of a whole turmeric extract. There is also some conjecture over using black pepper in place of turmeric's natural oils, since piperine interferes with one of the body's natural defences and can irritate the stomach.

Curcumin is only one of turmeric's 100-plus actives. Read the full explanation of turmeric versus curcumin.

Why does Complete 3 Turmeric™ contain ethanol, and how is it used?

As with most plant extracts, food-grade ethanol is the most efficient method of extracting turmeric's beneficial constituents from the fresh rhizome. It also enhances absorption, acts as a preservative and prevents microbial contamination. Food-grade ethanol is commonly used to produce pharmacy medicines such as cough mixtures, foods such as hamburger buns and kombucha, and flavourings such as vanilla essence.

Ethanol occurs naturally in ripening fruit. The recommended dose of Complete 3 Turmeric™ contains about the same amount of ethanol as three ripe bananas. The ethanol in Complete 3 Turmeric™ comes exclusively from plant sources and is certified organic, making it the only Australian turmeric extract that is 100% certified organic.

Taking and Storing

Dose, how to mix it, why liquid extracts behave differently, and how to keep your bottle in good condition.

What dose of Complete 3 Turmeric™ should I take?

Always read the label and use only as directed.

For general health maintenance, adults take 5–10 ml per day in 20–40 ml of liquid such as milk (dairy or non-dairy), juice or water, preferably with food. We recommend using a dropper or medicine glass to measure your dose.

For specific health conditions, it is advisable to check with your doctor or health care practitioner.

What advantages do liquid extracts have over capsules or tablets?

Liquid extracts have rapid bioavailability because they are quickly absorbed and digested. Compared to capsules or tablets, liquid extracts also retain the flavour and aroma of the plant they are made from.

Why might I notice sediment in my bottle?

You may notice a small amount of sediment that gradually settles at the bottom of the bottle. This is normal. It is a result of the extraction and filtering process and consists of microscopic particles of turmeric that can safely be consumed. For this reason, it is advisable to shake the bottle before pouring.

How should I store Complete 3 Turmeric™?

Keep Complete 3 Turmeric™ out of direct sunlight, preferably below 30 degrees Celsius, in a dark, cool place such as a medicine cabinet or kitchen pantry.

Quality and Regulation

Where Complete 3 Turmeric™ is grown and made, what an AUST L listing actually means, and how other products compare.

Where is Complete 3 Turmeric™ made?

Most of the hard work takes place on the farm, between planting and harvest. From that point, to become a listed medicine, the fresh turmeric rhizomes must be processed by a TGA licensed facility. Our facility is located in Australia and is also certified in accordance with the Australian Certified Organic (ACO) Standard.

Before release to market, Complete 3 Turmeric™ is analysed by TGA approved laboratories to ensure it has retained turmeric's natural components and is free from microbiological contamination.

Complete 3 Turmeric™ is a 'listed medicine'. What does this mean?

A listed medicine must meet the strict compliance and safety regulations of the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA). Compliance requires the compilation of credible scientific and/or traditional evidence to support its therapeutic claims. Listed medicines can only contain certain low risk ingredients and must be manufactured in a TGA licensed facility.

A unique 'AUST L' number is displayed on the label. Complete 3 Turmeric™ Liquid Extract is a listed medicine, number 286865.

What is the TGA?

The TGA, or Therapeutic Goods Administration, is part of the Australian Government's Health Department. It regulates the safety and manufacture of therapeutic goods such as prescription, pharmacy and complementary medicines.

Are all turmeric extracts sold in Australia listed medicines?

No. Many herbal extracts sold online and produced overseas do not have 'AUST L' numbers and do not meet the same standards as TGA listed medicines. There are also some extracts in capsule or powdered form that are really foods rather than therapeutic products.

What type of complementary medicines contain turmeric and curcumin?

There are over 200 products available over the counter or online in Australia with 'turmeric' and/or 'curcumin' on their labels. Most are standardised extracts in capsule or tablet form, and combine turmeric with other herbs, curcumin concentrates and/or fat and black pepper supplements.

Only two turmeric liquid extracts are listed, and Complete 3 Turmeric™ is the sole Australian product made with fresh certified organic turmeric.

Turmeric vs Curcumin

The short answers. The full explanation, covering black pepper, piperine, fats, concentrates and standardised versus full spectrum extracts, lives in our dedicated guide.

Is curcumin another name for turmeric?

No. Curcumin is one of turmeric's natural active compounds, making up about 5% of the total rhizome. Curcumin can be separated from turmeric and also manufactured synthetically.

The difference between 'curcumin' and 'turmeric' has become blurred, and the two are often referred to interchangeably in the media and in marketing campaigns. Read the full guide.

Why is curcumin poorly absorbed?

Curcumin is rapidly metabolised and excreted by a liver-induced process, which limits the amount of time it is available for absorption. This is the reason many products add black pepper, fats or curcumin concentrates. See how absorption works.

What is the difference between 'standardised' and 'full spectrum' extracts?

In standardised extracts, a plant's active compounds are adjusted to an identical proportion in each batch, typically above naturally occurring levels. Full spectrum extracts are made from a whole plant, or plant part, and contain all compounds in the same proportions as provided by nature.

Complete 3 Turmeric™ is a full spectrum extract. Compare the two approaches.
